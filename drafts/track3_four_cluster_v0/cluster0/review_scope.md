# Cluster 0: full-text methods, experiments and limitations review

Prepared 5 November 2026. Scope: all 23 papers assigned to Cluster 0. Every PDF was extracted in full and reviewed for research problem, method, data/tasks, experimental design, reported findings, author-stated limitations and implications for the proposed study. This is a source-grounded review, not an independent reproduction of the reported experiments or an exhaustive review outside the corpus.

The linked [claim-to-evidence table](claim_evidence_table.md) contains two short page-verified passages per paper. The table below paraphrases the full-text structured review; where interpretation extends beyond an author statement, it is explicitly identified as reviewer interpretation.

| [2025.emnlp-main.1063](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1063/) - Attention-guided Self-reflection for Zero-shot Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models | AGSER uses the original query, its generated answer, and internal attention maps from the generating LLM. It sums attention across heads from the final query token to each query token and, by default, averages these contributions across layers. Experiments compare AGSER with query–answer SBERT cosine similarity, SelfCheckGPT, INSIDE, and InterrogateLLM on Llama2-7b, Llama2-13b, Llama3-8b-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-14b-Instruct, using AUC. | PDF page 9: Dependence on internal attention restricts applicability to open-source LLMs and makes closed-source API models challenging. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim attention-guided, training-free query perturbation for hallucination detection as wholly new without distinguishing its contribution from AGSER. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1124](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1124/) - Unsupervised Hallucination Detection by Inspecting Reasoning Processes | IRIS prompts an accessible proxy LLM to verify a statement through step-by-step reasoning, extracts the verification response's last-token hidden state from the final layer, and trains a small three-hidden-layer MLP using uncertainty-derived soft pseudolabels. Each topic or sub-dataset receives an 80/20 training/test split, with accuracy on held-out sets as the main metric; the text also calls these validation sets. Main experiments use Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. | limitation: The evaluated instruction-tuned models span 500 million to 32 billion parameters; broader model families and sizes could provide additional insights.; pdf_page: 9 | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim novelty merely for unsupervised confidence-pseudolabeled probing of verification-response activations; IRIS already evaluates this combination. |

| [2025.emnlp-main.1239](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1239/) - Hallucination Detection in LLM s Using Spectral Features of Attention Maps | LapEigvals extracts attention maps from a single answer-generation run, including input and generated tokens. Five instruction-tuned open models are evaluated: Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B, Phi-3.5, Mistral-Nemo, and Mistral-Small-24B, primarily at temperatures 0.1 and 1.0. Each generated dataset receives a stratified 80/20 train/test split. | PDF page 9: Supervised training requires labeled hallucinated and non-hallucinated examples; LLM-generated labels may introduce noise or overfitting. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim to introduce the first supervised attention-map or Laplacian-spectral hallucination detector; LapEigvals is a relevant baseline. |

| [2025.emnlp-main.1452](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1452/) - Multi-Frequency Contrastive Decoding: Alleviating Hallucinations for Large Vision-Language Models | Multi-Frequency Contrastive Decoding (MFCD) combines next-token logits from the original image with negatively weighted logits from high-pass and low-pass versions of that image, conditioned on the same question and generated prefix. The authors compare MFCD with sampling, DoLa, VCD, and SID on LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, and Qwen2.5-VL for POPE; CHAIR and the main MME experiments use LLaVA-1.5. Sampling settings are shared across decoding comparisons: temperature 1.2, top-p 1.0, and top-k 50. | PDF page 9: MFCD only alleviates hallucination attributed to neglect of high- and low-frequency information; other causes include neglect of image information and incorrect user instructions. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal should not claim novelty merely for training-free frequency-based contrastive hallucination mitigation or adaptive use of distribution divergence and entropy; MFCD and MFCD-Plus already implement these ideas. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1481](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1481/) - How Much Do LLM s Hallucinate across Languages? Detector comparisons comprise 30 monolingual models versus one pooled multilingual model, binary versus category labeling, and bidirectional versus causal multilingual models, with three training seeds and token-level F1 reporting. | PDF p. 9: GPT-4-inserted synthetic hallucinations may not perfectly replicate natural model errors. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim multilingual span detection, translate-trained hallucination detection, or precision/recall-adjusted multilingual rate estimation as wholly new contributions without distinguishing this study. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.150](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.150/) - Active Layer-Contrastive Decoding Reduces Hallucination in Large Language Model Generation | ActLCD uses a binary policy to switch between greedy and layer-contrastive decoding at each generation step, using intermediate-layer embeddings and logits from the partial context. | [2025.emnlp-main.1515](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1515/) - Where Confabulation Lives: Latent Feature Discovery in LLM s | The method extracts white-box residual-stream activations, primarily at the final prompt position immediately before generation. | [2025.emnlp-main.1554](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1554/) - Estimating LLM Consistency: A User Baseline vs Surrogate Metrics | Participants rated response-pair semantic similarity on a six-point scale. Prompts were randomly assigned to Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Gemma-2-9B-it, or Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, with 33, 34, and 33 prompts respectively; temperatures remained at model defaults. | PDF page 9: Restricting experiments to open-weights models with accessible logits limits generalization to black-box models. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not describe this study’s human labels as factuality or hallucination labels, or its consistency correlations as hallucination-detection performance. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1651](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1651/) - P rune CD : Contrasting Pruned Self Model to Improve Decoding Factuality | PruneCD contrasts next-token log probabilities from the full model against those from the same model with selected decoder layers skipped. Layers are ranked by the decrease in TruthfulQA validation MC1 after individual ablation, and the top-k are pruned; an optional C4 perplexity-based filtering stage reduces the candidate set. The main comparisons use Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-3B/1B-Instruct against greedy decoding, DoLa, Activation Decoding, and END; appendices add Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and MCD comparisons. | PDF page 6: Batched inference may increase memory footprint through additional internal activations, particularly at very large batch sizes; the authors did not observe significant overhead in their small-batch evaluation setting. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not describe PruneCD as a trained hallucination detector or treat its factuality improvements as detection accuracy; a detection proposal needs explicit prediction units, label definitions, and discrimination/calibration metrics. |

Comparisons include probability/entropy baselines, Focus, CCP, SAR, semantic entropy, semantic density, lexical and graph-based sampling methods, and supervised Factoscope, SAPLMA, MIND, Sheeps. | PDF page 9: The method is supervised and benefits from task-specific training data. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim novelty merely for supervised hallucination detection using attention, token probabilities, or recurrent confidence propagation; TAD already combines these components. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1809](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1809/) - A Head to Predict and a Head to Question: Pre-trained Uncertainty Quantification Heads for Hallucination Detection in LLM Outputs | UHead concatenates token-level attention weights to a small window of preceding tokens across model layers and heads with top-token log probabilities. Detection compares SU alone, VU alone, and their combination using AUROC and accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. | PDF page 9: Evaluation is limited to short-form, sentence-length QA datasets, which may not capture real-world complexity. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim novelty merely for combining semantic and verbal uncertainty in hallucination detection or for probing question-final hidden states before generation. |

## Review boundaries

- Reported findings are author-reported unless independently reproduced; this package did not reproduce them.
- Full-paper extraction reduces abstract-only errors but does not guarantee that every methodological detail was captured.
- The two short evidence passages per paper are audit anchors, not substitutes for the complete paper.
- The candidate gap remains provisional until a broader database search beyond the ACL Anthology comparison set is completed.
