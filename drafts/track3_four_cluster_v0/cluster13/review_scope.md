# Cluster 13: full-text methods, experiments and limitations review

Prepared 5 November 2026. Scope: all 29 papers assigned to Cluster 13. Every PDF was extracted in full and reviewed for research problem, method, data/tasks, experimental design, reported findings, author-stated limitations and implications for the proposed study. This is a source-grounded review, not an independent reproduction of the reported experiments or an exhaustive review outside the corpus.

The linked [claim-to-evidence table](claim_evidence_table.md) contains two short page-verified passages per paper. The table below paraphrases the full-text structured review; where interpretation extends beyond an author statement, it is explicitly identified as reviewer interpretation.

| [2025.emnlp-main.1201](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1201/) - Investigating How Pre-training Data Leakage Affects Models’ Reproduction and Detection Capabilities | The study identifies leakage using regular expressions and named-entity recognition for personal information, web searches for copyrighted material, and exact matching against benchmark data. The authors evaluate 25 model variants across six families, comparing model sizes and available base/instruction-tuned pairs. Detection baselines are LOSS, PPL/zlib, Min-K%, and SaMIA with published default hyperparameters; Table 5 averages their results. | PDF page 9, Limitations: Sampling and identification through regular expressions, web searches, and databases restrict coverage to selected, typical leakage instances; exhaustive coverage of personal information, copyrighted texts, and benchmarks is. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not equate training-data membership with AI authorship, or cite the reported detection rates as human-versus-AI classification accuracy. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1227](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1227/) - What’s in a prompt? Language models encode literary style in prompt embeddings | Novels are tokenized and divided into non-overlapping fixed-length chunks, typically 8–128 tokens, without aligning sentence boundaries. The authors collect each chunk’s rightmost-token representation after successive transformer layers and probe its source using linear SVMs or a multiclass MLP. The main representation model is base Llama-3.2-1B. Binary probes use a linear SVM following PCA to 64 dimensions with a 70/30 training–validation split; the principal binary heatmap averages ten runs. | limitation: The Limitations section states that the study is restricted to open-source models in the 1–2 billion parameter range and a rather small text corpus; this stated model-size restriction is qualified by the Qwen3-14B experiment in Appendix D. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim novelty merely for probing final-token hidden representations to distinguish literary authors or GPT-4o from GPT-4.1; this paper already demonstrates those settings. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1265](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1265/) - Profiler: Black-box AI -generated Text Origin Detection via Context-aware Inference Pattern Analysis | PROFILER runs teacher-forced inference through open-source surrogate LLMs and computes cross-entropy losses between each output distribution and neighboring observed tokens within a symmetric context window, normally W=6. Evaluation uses one-versus-all origin classification and five-fold cross-validated average AUC for in-distribution tests, separately on original and paraphrased data. | PDF page 9: Detection relies on surrogate LLMs, and surrogate scale and quality may affect effectiveness, especially for highly capable or closely related generators such as distilled variants. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim that context-aware black-box generator attribution or provenance testing after paraphrasing is new; distinguish any proposed contribution from PROFILER's loss-window features and self-paraphrase evaluation. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1327](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1327/) - Speculating LLM s’ C hinese Training Data Pollution from Their Tokens | The authors define polluted Chinese (PoC) tokens using an expert-developed taxonomy covering adult content, online gambling, online games, online video, and anomalous content. POCDETECT fine-tunes GLM-4-32B with token strings, expert labels, and the top ten Google search results to classify tokens. A six-expert panel developed the taxonomy; annotation involved twelve native Chinese speakers, including those experts and six undergraduates. POCDETECT uses LoRA supervised fine-tuning of GLM-4-32B-0414 for two epochs. | limitation: Closed GPT-4o training data prevents ground-truth verification of its inferred pollution ratio.; pdf_page: 9 | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present POCDETECT as an AI-generated-text detector or treat its pollution labels as human/AI provenance labels. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1354](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1354/) - O pen T uring B ench: An Open-Model-based Benchmark and Framework for Machine-Generated Text Detection and Attribution | OpenTuringBench supplies supervised training data and seven evaluation tasks. OTBDetector encodes documents with Longformer, averages token embeddings, and uses triplet contrastive learning to bring texts sharing an authorship label together while separating different labels. The core collection is split approximately 80/10/10 into training, validation, and test sets; detectors train only on the core training split and are evaluated on task-specific test sets. | PDF page 9: News articles are the primary discourse domain; the authors explicitly recognize the need to expand their findings to other domains. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim that human–AI revision, continuation, same-generator rewriting, domain shift, or unseen-generator evaluation is novel merely by introducing these conditions; this paper already evaluates them. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1457](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1457/) - Large Language Models Threaten Language’s Epistemic and Communicative Foundations | The paper combines philosophical argument, discussion of prior literature, and proposed technical frameworks: hybrid authorship graphs linking humans, models, and documents; proof-of-interaction records; and authorship entropy, defined as Shannon entropy over possible authors conditional on a text. The empirical comparison is between a fixed human-authored unigram reference distribution and outputs across recursive GPT-2 fine-tuning iterations. The author reports increasing divergence but supplies no numerical results in the prose or detailed replication protocol. | PDF p. 9: Many proposed constructs remain speculative without empirical grounding, and quantitative metrics alone cannot capture the consequences of LLM saturation. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not cite this paper as an empirical detector benchmark or treat its 70% accuracy statement as a general performance ceiling. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1506](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1506/) - Machine-generated text detection prevents language model collapse | Each generation fine-tunes the original pretrained model on data produced by preceding generations, optionally mixed with human text and older synthetic samples. A binary encoder-based detector estimates machine-generation probability. The authors run 10 generations with one epoch of full fine-tuning per generation. Initial decoding comparisons use GPT-2 124M and SmolLM2 360M with greedy decoding, five-way beam search, pure sampling, temperature 0.9, top-k 50, and nucleus sampling 0.95. | PDF page 10: Experiments assess fine-tuning rather than pretraining from scratch because pretraining costs and complexity are impractical. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim novelty solely for using machine-generated-text detection to curate mixed training data or mitigate recursive model degradation; this paper already implements and evaluates that approach. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1567](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1567/) - S im M ark: A Robust Sentence-Level Similarity-Based Watermarking Algorithm for Large Language Models | SimMark generates sentences through black-box rejection sampling, accepting a candidate when its embedding similarity to the preceding sentence falls within a predefined interval, or accepting the last candidate when the retry budget expires. The authors report ROC-AUC and true-positive rates at nominal 1% and 5% false-positive rates, with all results from single runs. | PDF page 9: Sentence-level signals can be weaker and more variable in short generations; detection improves as more sentence-level evidence accumulates. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present watermark absence as evidence of human authorship; distinguish human text, unwatermarked AI text, and SimMark-watermarked text in labels and evaluation. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1595](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1595/) - Same Question, Different Words: A Latent Adversarial Framework for Prompt Robustness | Latent Adversarial Paraphrasing (LAP) alternates between learning input-specific hidden-state perturbations and updating the language model against them. Main comparisons use Llama-3-8b and Mistral-7b-v0.3, with an additional Llama-2-13b experiment. Baselines are supervised fine-tuning, data augmentation, prompt consistency, conventional latent adversarial training, self-refinement, universal self-consistency with four drafts. | PDF page 9: The study mainly considers text-modality prompt robustness; vision-language models and other modalities are left for future work. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not describe LAP's preference win-rates as detection accuracy, calibrated provenance probabilities, or evidence of low false-positive rates. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1607](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1607/) - T emp P araphraser: “Heating Up” Text to Evade AI -Text Detection through Paraphrasing | TempParaphraser fine-tunes a lightweight language model to reverse synthetic paraphrases back to human-written sentences. Training pairs originate from The Pile and are filtered using detector scores, semantic similarity, n-gram constraints, and rules. The main paraphraser is fully fine-tuned Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, evaluated at temperature 1.2 with one or seven candidates per sentence. | PDF page 9: Sentence-level processing may lose long-range contextual coherence in complex texts; the authors propose incorporating additional context during training and inference. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim that paraphrasing robustness, temperature sensitivity, or paraphrase-based detector augmentation is unexplored; distinguish the proposed contribution from these experiments. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.166](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.166/) - Are Generative Models Underconfident? Better Quality Estimation with Boosted Model Probability | BOOSTED PROB sorts the next-token probability distribution and identifies a dominant cluster using the last probability drop exceeding both relative and absolute thresholds. For a selected token inside this cluster, its quality score becomes the cluster's total probability mass; otherwise, its original probability is retained. Models include Whisper Large V3 and smaller Whisper variants, fine-tuned DeltaLM Large, NLLB 3.3B, Tower 7B, Bloomz 560M, Llama 3.2 3B, and 4-bit Llama 3.3 Instruct 70B. | PDF page 9, Section 8: BOOSTED PROB does not work well with very weak models and may emphasize their overconfidence; the authors recommend a stronger external scoring model. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not cite this paper as demonstrating human-versus-AI detection or authorship attribution; quality labels and provenance labels require separate operational definitions. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1665](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1665/) - From Shortcuts to Balance: Attribution Analysis of Speech-Text Feature Utilization in Distinguishing Original from Machine-Translated Texts | The framework uses Integrated Gradients with 50 integration steps, zero text-embedding baselines, and silence audio baselines. In bimodal models, each modality is attributed while the other remains fixed. The authors compare standard and NE-masked fine-tuning of DeBERTa-v3-large for English and MacBERT-large for Chinese; frozen versus unfrozen HuBERT classifiers, with feature-extraction layers always frozen; and MISA fusion of NE-masked text encoders with frozen HuBERT. | Only one translation system is analyzed; the authors propose investigating additional NMT architectures to assess variation in feature utilization (PDF page 5). | Reviewer interpretation: Do not equate original-versus-machine-translated labels with general human-versus-AI authorship, or claim detection of mixed authorship, post-editing, or paraphrasing from these experiments. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1766](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1766/) - Leveraging Multilingual Training for Authorship Representation: Enhancing Generalization across Languages and Domains | The main model fine-tunes XLM-RoBERTa-large using supervised contrastive learning on same-author document pairs. Probabilistic content masking randomly masks lower-frequency subword tokens, treating high-frequency tokens as approximate function tokens to encourage stylistic rather than topical representations. Authorship data use 85/5/10 training/validation/test splits for seen subsets and 33.3/66.7 validation/test splits for unseen subsets. | PDF page 9: Each author is restricted to writing in one language; accurate attribution across languages for multilingual authors remains unclear and is left for future work. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim that multilingual authorship representations have never been evaluated for AI-generated text detection; this paper evaluates that combination on MULTITuDE. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1783](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1783/) - so much depends / upon / a whitespace: Why Whitespace Matters for Poets and LLM s | The authors introduce WISP, a typology covering line breaks, prefix spacing, internal spacing, vertical spacing, and line length. Their computational analyses focus on the first three categories, comparing whitespace lengths and syntactic dependencies across sources, forms, topics, and poet birth decades. Each generator receives either a prompt explicitly requesting creative whitespace or a simplified prompt without that request. Comparisons include published versus Reddit free verse, generator and prompt conditions, poetic forms, topic tags, and historical cohorts. | limitation: The linguistic and whitespace analysis covers only English-language poems in Roman script and may not transfer to other languages or scripts.; pdf_page: 10 | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present this study as demonstrating an effective AI-poetry detector or calibrated authorship probabilities; its benchmark measures extraction fidelity, not detection. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1784](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1784/) - Certified Mitigation of Worst-Case LLM Copyright Infringement | BLOOM SCRUB indexes normalized character n-grams from the protected corpus in a Bloom filter, extracts overlapping matching spans from generated responses, and repeatedly prompts a rewrite model to paraphrase detected quotations. Experiments use 25-character indexing and up to five rewrites. For each corpus, the authors fully fine-tune Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for three epochs as the generator and use the off-the-shelf version as the rewriter, with greedy decoding throughout. | PDF page 9: Eliminating direct quotations is insufficient to mitigate copyright risk fully; non-literal copying, including paraphrased or stylistically similar outputs, remains an open challenge. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not equate corpus quotation detection with AI-authorship attribution; define source overlap, provenance, and human/AI authorship as distinct targets. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1789](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1789/) - Retracing the Past: LLM s Emit Training Data When They Get Lost | Confusion-Inducing Attacks (CIA) optimize input snippets using Greedy Coordinate Gradient to maximize average predictive entropy across snippet positions. Unaligned models are Llama 1 65B, Llama 2 70B, and OLMo 7B; aligned models are Llama 2-Chat 70B, Llama 3-Instruct 70B, and Llama 3.1-Instruct 8B. | PDF page 9: Universal sufficiency of the high-entropy precursor for memorization is not established. | Reviewer interpretation: Define generation mechanism, original source authorship, and document editing history separately; corpus overlap alone must not be treated as an AI-authorship label. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.183](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.183/) - OBLIVIATE : Robust and Practical Machine Unlearning for Large Language Models | OBLIVIATE uses GPT-4o, seeded with initial target terms, to identify additional target tokens. The authors compare against original models and dataset-specific baselines including WHP, RMU, ELM, gradient ascent, random labels, NPO and its KL/GD variants, adversarial GA/NPO, gradient difference, preference optimization, and KL minimization. | PDF page 10: The largest tested model is Llama3-8B-Instruct; scalability to larger models and applicability to additional datasets, including news/article corpora, require further study. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not cite this paper as an AI-text detector or treat membership inference, DRMA, or TOFU fictional-author profiles as human/AI authorship labels. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.268](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.268/) - S en D et EX : Sentence-Level AI -Generated Text Detection for Human- AI Hybrid Content via Style and Context Fusion | AutoFill-Refine randomly masks sentences in human documents, prompts an autoregressive LLM to fill them, and retains completed documents only when their LLaMA-3-8B perplexity is lower than the original document's. | [2025.emnlp-main.294](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.294/) - M o SE s: Uncertainty-Aware AI -Generated Text Detection via Mixture of Stylistics Experts with Conditional Thresholds | MoSEs wraps an existing discrimination-score model. The authors compare MoSEs-lr and MoSEs-xg against reference-set static thresholds optimized for Youden's J and nearest voting over 100 discrimination scores. Score models are RoBERTa-base, Fast-DetectGPT, and Lastde, with GPT-Neo-2.7B used by the latter two. | PDF page 9: Excessive PCA compression can lose deep semantic information, whereas retaining too many dimensions can introduce noise and destabilize detection accuracy. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not equate binary corpus source labels with verified individual authorship, misconduct, or complete provenance; it should explicitly define how human-prompted continuations, mixed authorship, and edited documents are labeled. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.370](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.370/) - Context-Aware Membership Inference Attacks against Pre-trained Large Language Models | CAMIA extracts signals from per-token prediction losses: token-diversity-calibrated loss, early-token cut-off loss, loss slope, robust low-loss counts, fluctuation complexity, and changes induced by repeating the input. | [2025.emnlp-main.379](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.379/) - Genre Matters: How Text Types Interact with Decoding Strategies and Lexical Predictors in Shaping Reading Behavior | The authors analyze EMTeC eye-tracking data using linear mixed-effects regressions for log-transformed reading durations and logistic mixed-effects regressions for binary reading measures. ## Review boundaries

- Reported findings are author-reported unless independently reproduced; this package did not reproduce them.
- Full-paper extraction reduces abstract-only errors but does not guarantee that every methodological detail was captured.
- The two short evidence passages per paper are audit anchors, not substitutes for the complete paper.
- The candidate gap remains provisional until a broader database search beyond the ACL Anthology comparison set is completed.
