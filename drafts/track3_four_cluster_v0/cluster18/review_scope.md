# Cluster 18: full-text methods, experiments and limitations review

Prepared 5 November 2026. Scope: all 16 papers assigned to Cluster 18. Every PDF was extracted in full and reviewed for research problem, method, data/tasks, experimental design, reported findings, author-stated limitations and implications for the proposed study. This is a source-grounded review, not an independent reproduction of the reported experiments or an exhaustive review outside the corpus.

The linked [claim-to-evidence table](claim_evidence_table.md) contains two short page-verified passages per paper. The table below paraphrases the full-text structured review; where interpretation extends beyond an author statement, it is explicitly identified as reviewer interpretation.

| Paper | Method and experimental scope reviewed | Author-stated limitation reviewed | Implication for this proposal - reviewer interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [2025.emnlp-main.1065](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1065/) - Understanding and Mitigating Overrefusal in LLM s from an Unveiling Perspective of Safety Decision Boundary | RASS generates multilingual toxic seeds with Mixtral 8×7B Instruct, rewrites them into intended-benign probes using language-conditioned templates, and moderates remaining toxicity. Benchmark comparisons contrast RASS-selected prompts with the original moderated pool constructed using the OR-Bench methodology. | PDF page 3: Generated seed prompts may inherit biases from the underlying LLM; the authors prioritize generation efficiency and recommend ensemble generation, human-in-the-loop curation, or adversarial filtering. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim that multilingual overrefusal benchmarking or representation-guided boundary sampling is new without distinguishing its contribution from RASS and MORBench. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1091](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1091/) - Interpretation Meets Safety: A Survey on Interpretation Methods and Tools for Improving LLM Safety | The authors curate nearly 70 works at the intersection of interpretation, safety enhancement, and tools, organizing interpretation by training processes, input tokens, inference-time internals, and generated self-reasoning. They connect these categories to six safety enhancement strategies and four visualization tool types. This paper introduces no original model experiment, baseline comparison, quantitative meta-analysis, or human evaluation. | Some recent advancements may be missing because the field is extensive and rapidly evolving (PDF page 10, Section 8). | Reviewer interpretation: Do not cite this survey as experimental proof that interpretation-informed guardrails improve multilingual safety or reduce over-refusal. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1392](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1392/) - M r G uard: A Multilingual Reasoning Guardrail for Universal LLM Safety | MrGuard combines GPT-4o-mini-generated translations and bilingual safety explanations, supervised fine-tuning, and curriculum-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Translations retain English seed labels only when GPT reassessment agrees. The main models are LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct trained with QLoRA; supplementary experiments use Qwen2.5-3B. | limitation: Budget and computational constraints restrict synthetic training data to high- and mid-resource languages and a single English seed dataset, Aegis-2.0; evaluation language coverage also remains limited.; pdf_page: 9 | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal should not claim multilingual reasoning guardrails, culturally enriched curricula, code-switching robustness tests, or native-reviewed synthetic-label preservation as unexplored contributions without comparison to this work. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1501](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1501/) - How Does DPO Reduce Toxicity? A Mechanistic Neuron-Level Analysis | The authors train linear toxicity probes on final-layer residual representations, project activation-weighted MLP value vectors onto those probes, and compare pre-training and post-DPO states. Counterfactual activation patching tests selected neurons' contributions. Experiments cover pretrained GPT-2-Medium, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-2B, and Mistral-7B. Comparisons include untreated models, DPO, final-layer probe steering, patching interpretable toxic neurons or the 256 most toxic-aligned neurons, sequential patching of the four identified. | PDF page 9: A single linear probe aggregates toxicity, whereas different aspects such as hate speech and abusive language may require a multidimensional subspace; preliminary subspace analysis produces inconsistent neuron alignments and projection changes. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present distributed activation editing or the four-group account of DPO toxicity reduction as novel without distinguishing the proposed contribution from this study. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1521](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1521/) - Sparse Autoencoder Features for Classifications and Transferability | The pipeline extracts residual-stream activations and pretrained Gemma Scope SAE features, optionally retains the top 20 or 50 activations per token, sums activations across tokens, and optionally binarizes aggregated features at a threshold greater than 1. The authors evaluate Gemma 2 2B, 9B, and 9B-IT at early, middle, and late layers, using 16K/65K SAE widths for 2B and 16K/131K for 9B variants. | PDF page 9: Primary analysis is restricted to Jump ReLU SAEs on Gemma 2, potentially limiting generalization to other architectures and training paradigms. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not equate toxicity classification F1 with reduced harmful compliance or reduced over-refusal; evaluate generated refusal/compliance behavior separately. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1528](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1528/) - GRAID : Synthetic Data Generation with Geometric Constraints and Multi-Agentic Reflection for Harmful Content Detection | GRAID combines two stages. First, a LoRA-tuned Llama 3.1 8B Instruct receives a class label and a target vector formed from summed token embeddings; a custom loss combines cross-entropy with a token-to-target distance penalty. ModernBERT-Large and RoBERTa-Large are trained on original data, original plus geometric augmentation, and original plus geometric and reflective augmentation. A size-matched Reflective* baseline samples raw generation outputs without reflective constraint checks. | PDF page 9: The reflective pipeline incurs non-negligible computation through repeated LLM calls; the authors report approximately five A10 GPU hours for geometric augmentation and ten for reflective augmentation. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not cite these results as evidence of multilingual safety improvement; evaluate specified languages, dialects, registers, and code-switching with qualified native review. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1677](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1677/) - Language Models Identify Ambiguities and Exploit Loopholes | The study supplies agents with competing goals and ambiguous requests, measuring outputs that comply pragmatically, exploit a literal loophole, or fail to comply. It tests scalar implicature, logical bracketing, and interpersonal power scenarios. Models include GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Llama-3.1-Instruct (8B, 70B), Qwen-2.5-Instruct (3B–72B), and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (1.5B–32B). Each prompt is run 10 times at temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.95, averaging across three seeds. | PDF page 10: Observations are limited to model outputs; generated reasoning may not faithfully explain behavior, and the authors do not establish genuine beliefs about intent or moral agency. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not cite this study as evidence of cross-lingual safety disparities, native-speaker validation, or multilingual over-refusal; those require separate measurements. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1781](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1781/) - A Simple Yet Effective Method for Non-Refusing Context Relevant Fine-grained Safety Steering in LLM s | SAFE STEER computes layer-specific differences between mean attention activations for harmful-category inputs and harmless inputs, then applies scaled steering vectors during generation. Generic harmless data can replace category-matched contrastive examples. Models are Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, and aligned Llama-2-7B-chat, with some inconsistent base/instruct naming across the text. Comparisons include unsteered generation, Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA), and Spectral Editing of Activations (SEA). | limitation: Only a subset of harm categories from two datasets is evaluated; broader coverage is needed to establish generality.; pdf_page: 9 | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present non-refusing safety steering or generic-safe-data vector construction as wholly new; compare against SAFE STEER under matched models, extraction data, and intervention budgets. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1788](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1788/) - Not Your Typical Government Tipline: LLM -Assisted Routing of Environmental Protection Agency Citizen Tips | The pipeline uses two separately LoRA-fine-tuned Mistral-7B classifiers trained on free-text submissions: a relevance filter followed by civil-versus-criminal triage. Confidence scores come from the first generated token, with adjustable decision thresholds optimized for F1 or illustrative asymmetric error costs. The authors describe a 70/10/20 training/validation/test split and five-fold cross-validation with independently optimized thresholds, reporting means and standard deviations. | limitation: A field trial is needed to measure asymmetric error costs and processing-time effects; without those measurements, optimization metrics may not match the agency's implicit utility function.; pdf_page: 6 | Reviewer interpretation: Do not describe jurisdictional filtering errors as measured LLM refusals or treat criminal/civil labels as harmful/benign request labels. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.197](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.197/) - DSCD : Large Language Model Detoxification with Self-Constrained Decoding | DSCD contrasts intermediate-layer next-token distributions within the same model. It identifies a coarse toxic layer through differences between safe and unsafe response hidden states, selects token-dependent safety and hallucination layers using Jensen–Shannon divergence, and contrasts the final-layer distribution against a. The study evaluates Llama2-7b-chat, Llama2-7b-uncensored-chat, Mistral-7b-v0.1, and Qwen2-7b-instruct against vanilla generation, SFT, DPO, DINM, and SafeDecoding, including DSCD combinations. | PDF page 9: Time and resource constraints prevented generalization testing with more detoxification methods. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim that training-free decoding guardrails or combined harmful/benign evaluations are novel in themselves; compare against DSCD and relevant hybrid baselines. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.298](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.298/) - Detoxifying Large Language Models via the Diversity of Toxic Samples | DivDetox combines Multi-Category-Induced Personalized Sample Generation (MPSG) with Scaled Contrastive Direct Preference Optimization (SC-DPO). MPSG elicits model-generated negatives using prompts and examples spanning 11 MLCommons harm categories, while unprompted generations supply positive candidates. Main models are GPT2-Large, Pythia-1.4B/2.8B/6.9B, and Llama-3-8B, with extensions to Llama-2-13B and Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Available baselines include original models, DEXPERTS, ToxiReversal, AURA, DPO, and Llama-3-8B-Instruct. | limitation: The method focuses exclusively on toxicity mitigation; sentiment control and specific-information removal are future extensions.; pdf_page: 9 | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim novelty merely for multi-category toxic prompting, self-generated model-specific negatives, or multi-negative preference optimization; DivDetox already combines these elements. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.41](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.41/) - Automating Steering for Safe Multimodal Large Language Models | AutoSteer selects a probing layer using the Safety Awareness Score (SAS), calculated from cosine similarities among activation differences for controlled toxic–safe pairs. A separately trained, lightweight MLP predicts binary input toxicity from that layer. The authors compare original LLaVA-OV-7B and Chameleon-7B, unconditional LM-Steer, and AutoSteer, principally at steering intensity 0.1. SAS selects layers 20 and 24 respectively. | PDF page 9: Prober generalization may be limited by curated training-data quality and diversity, particularly for out-of-distribution harms and novel adversarial strategies. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present selective inference-time multimodal steering with a trained toxicity probe and refusal head as an entirely new mechanism. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.49](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.49/) - Refusal-Aware Red Teaming: Exposing Inconsistency in Safety Evaluations | The framework trains a red-team LLM with PPO to maximize a weighted refusal-gap reward. Internal refusal is estimated through hidden-state refusal directions or a linear classifier and compared with thresholded external safety scores. Adaptive weights emphasize less frequent over-refusal or under-refusal cases. RL+HCuriosity is compared with zero-shot, few-shot, RL, RL+TDiv, and RL+Curiosity methods. RL methods use PPO initialized from LLaMA-3-8B-Lexic-Uncensored; an initialization comparison also uses Meta-Llama-3-8B. The reported setup does not clearly identify the target LLM. | PDF page 9: Effectiveness depends on the evaluator, which can produce false positives or false negatives on adversarial harmful queries; generalization to evolving attacks remains challenging. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not treat model–evaluator disagreement as confirmed model error; independently adjudicate prompt harmfulness and response appropriateness, including evaluator mistakes. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.612](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.612/) - Toxicity Red-Teaming: Benchmarking LLM Safety in S ingapore’s Low-Resource Languages | SGToxicGuard adapts HateCheck and SGHateCheck into three adversarial tasks: single-turn Toxic Conversation, hateful fill-in-the-blank Toxic QA, and Toxic Tweet Composition. The authors compare Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, SeaLLM-v3-7B-Chat, SEA-LION-v2-Instruct, and GPT-4o-mini. | PDF page 9, Limitations: The LLM judge may make incorrect judgments when evaluating conversation and composition. The authors defend its scalability and cite agreement with a manually evaluated subset as mitigation. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim that Singapore-localized multilingual toxicity red-teaming or comparisons across conversation, QA, and composition are novel without distinguishing the proposed contribution from SGToxicGuard. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.641](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.641/) - Breaking Bad Tokens: Detoxification of LLM s Using Sparse Autoencoders | Pretrained residual-stream SAEs identify toxicity-associated features, whose activations are ablated or whose decoder vectors are subtracted during generation. Steering is constant, conditional on an input triggering a feature, or conditional on individual tokens triggering features. Strengths range from 0.5 to 2.5. Experiments compare unmodified models, feature ablation, and three steering strategies on GPT-2 Small at layers 5/10 and Gemma-2-2B at layers 10/20, with supplementary Gemma-2-2B-IT tests. GPT-2 SAEs have approximately 25K features; Gemma comparisons use 16K and 65K widths. | PDF page 9: Only GPT-2 Small and Gemma-2-2B(-IT) are studied; transfer to larger contemporary chat models and other model families remains unresolved. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim this paper demonstrates multilingual safety or culturally valid harm labels; evaluate languages and registers explicitly with native or culturally competent reviewers. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.819](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.819/) - MULTIGUARD : An Efficient Approach for AI Safety Moderation Across Languages and Modalities | OMNIGUARD selects a model layer using a Universality Score: mean cosine similarity of aligned translation or cross-modal pairs minus similarity of mismatched pairs. The main outcome is input harmfulness-classification accuracy, not generated-response refusal or compliance. Text comparisons include LlamaGuard variants, AegisGuard, WildGuard, HarmBench classifiers, MD-Judge, DuoGuard, and PolyGuard; DuoGuard and PolyGuard are also. | PDF page 9: Performance depends on the underlying model's understanding of the language, image, or audio; failures of understanding may prevent harmfulness detection. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim that multilingual or multimodal representation-based guardrails are unexplored; it should compare against this layer-selection and lightweight-classifier approach. |

## Review boundaries

- Reported findings are author-reported unless independently reproduced; this package did not reproduce them.
- Full-paper extraction reduces abstract-only errors but does not guarantee that every methodological detail was captured.
- The two short evidence passages per paper are audit anchors, not substitutes for the complete paper.
- The candidate gap remains provisional until a broader database search beyond the ACL Anthology comparison set is completed.
