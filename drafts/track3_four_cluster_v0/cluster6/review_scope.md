# Cluster 6: full-text methods, experiments and limitations review

Prepared 5 November 2026. Scope: all 17 papers assigned to Cluster 6. Every PDF was extracted in full and reviewed for research problem, method, data/tasks, experimental design, reported findings, author-stated limitations and implications for the proposed study. This is a source-grounded review, not an independent reproduction of the reported experiments or an exhaustive review outside the corpus.

The linked [claim-to-evidence table](claim_evidence_table.md) contains two short page-verified passages per paper. The table below paraphrases the full-text structured review; where interpretation extends beyond an author statement, it is explicitly identified as reviewer interpretation.

| Paper | Method and experimental scope reviewed | Author-stated limitation reviewed | Implication for this proposal - reviewer interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [2025.emnlp-main.1152](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1152/) - Transitive self-consistency evaluation of NLI models without gold labels | The method replaces one noun, verb, adjective, or adverb in an NLI hypothesis with an antonym. The WordNet pipeline matches part of speech, selects an antonym using contextual Word2Vec cosine similarity, checks grammar, and filters modifications with a CoLA acceptability classifier; an alternative pipeline prompts LLMs for. The main consistency experiments compare BART-large-MNLI, RoBERTa-large-MNLI, DeBERTa-large-MNLI, and ModernBERT-base-nli across datasets and rules using WordNet-generated examples. | PDF page 9: Some antonym replacements produce entailments rather than contradictions, so the replacement procedure requires further exploration and possible enhancement. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not equate transitive label consistency with explanation faithfulness; it should define and independently test the relationship between explanations and model decisions. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1231](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1231/) - LLM s Don’t Know Their Own Decision Boundaries: The Unreliability of Self-Generated Counterfactual Explanations | The authors enumerate complete, discrete tabular input spaces and translate their instances into natural-language prediction prompts. Predictions over every instance provide a lookup table defining each model’s decision boundary. The main comparison is unconstrained versus minimal counterfactual prompting across Gemma 2 27B, Llama 3.3 70B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama 70B, Claude Sonnet 3.7 non-thinking, GPT-4.1 and o3. | PDF page 10, Section 7: Mean excess distance characterises valid counterfactuals only and does not capture how close invalid outputs lie to the decision boundary. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim that validity alone establishes informative or minimal counterfactual explanations; evaluate both and state the distance definition. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1319](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1319/) - Rule Discovery for Natural Language Inference Data Generation Using Out-of-Distribution Detection | A BERT-base classifier trained on 15,000 rule-generated examples identifies SNLI pairs outside existing rules using maximum softmax probability, temperature scaling, and embedding-level input preprocessing. OOD comparisons include MSP, MSP plus temperature scaling, the full MSP+TS+IP method, and Monte Carlo Dropout. Clustering compares premise–hypothesis pairs, explanation-augmented triples, and explanations alone; subsequent experiments vary cluster-selection depth and. | limitation: Automated rule discovery still depends on similarity thresholds and filtering criteria; removing these dependencies remains an open challenge despite additional threshold experiments.; pdf_page: 9 | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must distinguish causal explanation faithfulness from this paper's explanation usefulness, rule overlap, and semantic-consistency criteria; it should not present this study as direct validation of faithful CoT reasoning. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1343](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1343/) - Sentence Smith: Controllable Edits for Evaluating Text Embeddings | Sentence Smith parses English sentences into AMR using off-the-shelf BART-based models, applies human-designed graph transformations, and generates text from the modified graph. The demonstration uses polarity negation, random node swaps, leaf-node deletion, WordNet antonym replacement, and WordNet hypernym substitution. The authors evaluate 29 off-the-shelf embedding models, including SBERT-derived models, LaBSE, E5, Jina, and leaderboard-selected models, plus separate probes of E5-Mistral-7B and GTE-Qwen-7B embeddings. | limitation: AMR has monolinguality, ambiguity-related meaning non-isomorphisms, and incomplete treatment of scope and tense.; pdf_page: 9 | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present this paper as demonstrating faithful explanations of embedding-model reasoning; it demonstrates transparent input transformations and targeted behavioral evaluation. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1462](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1462/) - Explaining Differences Between Model Pairs in Natural Language through Sample Learning | SLED optimizes randomly initialized inputs through differentiable classifiers to obtain convergent and divergent synthetic samples, using cross-entropy objectives and Mahalanobis-distance regularization relative to 10 reference inputs. Results are aggregated over 130 runs per structured task and 100 per text task, varying classifier configurations and training subsets. | PDF page 9: SLED applies only to differentiable models because synthetic sample learning requires gradient-based optimization. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not equate agreement/divergence prediction accuracy with causal or mechanistic faithfulness; add targeted feature interventions if making causal claims. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1479](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1479/) - Do LLM s Behave as Claimed? Investigating How LLM s Follow Their Own Claims using Counterfactual Questions | Behave as Claimed (BaC) asks whether the model could answer a target question correctly under a specified demonstration-removal intervention, then implements that intervention in a separate verification input. The main table compares Mistral-7B, Llama3-8B, Llama3.1-8B, Yi-1.5-6B, Qwen2-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, InternLM2.5-7B, a model labeled Qwen2.5-13B, GPT-4o, and GPT-4.1; three smaller models also receive CoT prompting. | PDF page 9: BaC has not yet been applied to long-term or multi-turn interactions. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim novelty for checking prospective model claims against actual outputs after demonstration-removal interventions without distinguishing the proposal from BaC. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1494](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1494/) - Graph-Guided Textual Explanation Generation Framework | G-TEx first trains a label-only model, then extracts attention-based token highlights, cross-input token interactions, or span interactions obtained using Louvain community detection. Main experiments compare T5-large and BART-large self-rationalization against fine-tuning without highlights and prompt-based fine-tuning using the same highlights as G-TEx; GCN, GAT, and GraphSAGE variants are compared. Supplementary experiments use LED and Llama-3.2-1B. | PDF page 9: Computational constraints restrict the main experiments to T5-large and BART-large; effects of model scalability on NLE quality warrant further study. | Reviewer interpretation: A new proposal must not claim that graph-guided use of token or interaction attributions for NLE generation is unexplored; G-TEx already implements and evaluates this approach. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1496](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1496/) - A Causal Lens for Evaluating Faithfulness Metrics | CAUSAL DIAGNOSTICITY constructs edited model variants that should produce the same answer using different knowledge, then evaluates whether a metric ranks the explanation aligned with the evaluated variant above its paired alternative. Main experiments compare post-hoc Simulatability and CC-SHAP with CoT Early Answering, Filler Tokens, Adding Mistakes, Paraphrasing, and CC-SHAP on Qwen2.5-7B and Gemma-2-9B-IT. Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct serves as simulator and corruption helper. | PDF page 5: Synthetic explanations reduce realism, although the authors use them to emphasize edited knowledge and support the intended faithful–unfaithful labels. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal should not present metric meta-evaluation using answer-preserving knowledge edits as wholly novel without distinguishing its contribution from CAUSAL DIAGNOSTICITY. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1615](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1615/) - Extractive Fact Decomposition for Interpretable Natural Language Inference in one Forward Pass | JEDI jointly extracts premise fact spans and classifies their relationship to an undecomposed hypothesis using a single contextual encoder pass. A global classifier first gates inference; entailment or contradiction is returned only when a classified span agrees with the global prediction, otherwise the output is neutral. Experiments use DeBERTaBASE and DeBERTaLARGE, comparing JEDI with ordinary sequence classification, SenLR, sentence-boundary-based JEDIsent, hypothesis-span SLR-NLI, generative-fact FGLR, and synthetic-rationale token classifier SYRPFT. | PDF page 9: Interpretability is limited to premise decomposition; extending the method to multi-sentence hypotheses would require hypothesis decomposition and appropriate supervision. | Reviewer interpretation: A new proposal should not claim novelty merely for encoder-only joint extractive fact decomposition, span-wise NLI, or requiring global–span label agreement. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.1728](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1728/) - L i TE x: A Linguistic Taxonomy of Explanations for Understanding Within-Label Variation in Natural Language Inference | LITEX classifies explicitly verbalized explanations into eight categories: Coreference, Syntactic, Semantic, Pragmatic, Absence of Mention, Logic Conflict, Factual Knowledge, and Inferential Knowledge. Classification compares random and majority baselines with fine-tuned BERT-base and RoBERTa-base and prompted Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o, and DeepSeek-v3; an appendix additionally evaluates LoRA fine-tuning of Llama-3.2-3B. | PDF page 9: Main annotations depend on a single annotator's subjective interpretation, particularly for borderline categories such as Factual versus Inferential Knowledge. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not equate this paper's gold-label support judgments with evidence that generated explanations faithfully expose a model's internal decision process; a causal-faithfulness proposal should separately test prediction–explanation dependence. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.396](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.396/) - Can LLM s Explain Themselves Counterfactually? | A three-step protocol obtains the model's original prediction, requests a revised input targeting a different prediction, and re-evaluates the revision using the same model. Generation strategies are unconstrained revision, identification and revision of prediction rationales, and CoT prompting. Comparisons span seven models: Gemma-2-9B-it and 27B-it, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct-2501, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B. | PDF page 9: Experiments are restricted to classification and integer-answer mathematics because automatically judging validity for open-ended generation is difficult; extending evaluation would require substantial human annotation. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not claim novelty merely for generating self-counterfactual inputs, testing whether they induce target predictions, or comparing evaluation with and without conversation history; this paper already implements those comparisons. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.427](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.427/) - Self-Critique and Refinement for Faithful Natural Language Explanations | SR-NLE first generates an answer and then an explanation, holding the answer fixed during up to three explanation-refinement rounds. Feedback is either newly generated natural-language self-critique each round or a fixed list of five important input words reused across rounds. The authors test Falcon3-Instruct 7B, Llama3.1-Instruct 8B, Mistral-Instruct-v0.3 7B, and Qwen2.5-Instruct 7B, using greedy decoding except for the sampling baseline. | PDF page 10: Experiments cover only post-hoc explanations; generalization to jointly generated rationales or chain-of-thought reasoning remains unclear. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim that self-critique or attribution-guided refinement for post-hoc explanation faithfulness is unexplored; SR-NLE already implements and evaluates these approaches. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.512](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.512/) - F in T rust: A Comprehensive Benchmark of Trustworthiness Evaluation in Finance Domain | FINTRUST evaluates seven trustworthiness dimensions using task-specific metrics and predominantly GPT-4.1 mini judging. Its transparency task crosses assigned ownership of Company A or B with the presence or absence of explicit transparency instructions, measuring selection of the preferred investment and ownership disclosure. The authors compare GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1 mini, o4-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Qwen3, Llama 4 Maverick, DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3, Fin-R1, XuanYuan-Fin-X1, and DianJin-R1, using temperature 0.8 when available. | PDF page 9: The authors do not perform instruction fine-tuning or alignment on FINTRUST and therefore cannot assess whether targeted alignment improves transparency or other trustworthiness dimensions. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal should not claim to introduce the first finance-specific ownership-intervention or conflict-disclosure evaluation; FINTRUST already implements these comparisons. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.529](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.529/) - Towards Faithful Natural Language Explanations: A Study Using Activation Patching in Large Language Models | Causal Faithfulness (CaF) compares activation-patching attribution matrices for the generated answer and its post-hoc explanation. Symmetric Token Replacement creates an equal-token-length counterfactual input; clean hidden states are then patched into corrupted runs at individual token–layer locations. Six Gemma-2 models are compared: pretrained and instruction-tuned variants at 2B, 9B, and 27B parameters. Pretrained models receive three-shot prompts, while chat models are evaluated zero-shot; scores are averaged over three seeds. | PDF page 9: CaF requires T × L forward passes, increasing computational requirements relative to surface-level tests. | Reviewer interpretation: A new proposal must not claim novelty merely for comparing answer and explanation causal attributions, incorporating layer-wise alignment, or using activation patching to evaluate post-hoc explanation faithfulness. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.575](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.575/) - Deriving Strategic Market Insights with Large Language Models: A Benchmark for Forward Counterfactual Generation | FIN-FORCE evaluates generated scenarios using forward-compatibility, directionality, and GPT-2-based delta perplexity. Forward-compatibility requires logically connected progression without contradicting the original event; directionality requires a substantial, financially plausible positive or negative shift with broader. Zero-shot and few-shot baselines include Claude 3.5 Haiku, Gemini 2.0 Flash, GPT-4o, Llama 4 Maverick, and Qwen 2.5 72B; few-shot results average five example samplings, with examples held consistent across models. | PDF page 9: The benchmark is limited to English-language headlines, restricting coverage of regions where English financial news sources are limited. | Reviewer interpretation: Do not present this benchmark's counterfactual scenarios as faithful explanations of a financial predictor; a proposal making that claim needs a specified target decision and tests linking explanations to the predictor's behavior. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.736](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.736/) - Multi-Domain Explainability of Preferences | Gemini-1.5-Pro discovers concepts from task- and subdomain-grouped preference examples, filters semantic duplicates, defines concepts, and annotates response pairs. Representations are either ternary concept-wise comparisons or differences between independently assigned 1–7 scores. Prediction evaluation averages 25 in-domain splits with 2,800 training and 400 test instances, and uses leave-one-domain-out evaluation across five seeds per domain, with five-fold hyperparameter tuning. | limitation: The linear HMDR model may not capture complex nonlinear human or LLM preference processes; linearity is also a deliberate interpretability choice.; pdf_page: 10 | Reviewer interpretation: A new proposal must not present automated concept discovery, shared/domain-specific preference surrogates, concept-guided generation, or explanation-guided tie resolution as unexplored solely on the basis of this paper. |
| [2025.emnlp-main.797](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.797/) - A Necessary Step toward Faithfulness: Measuring and Improving Consistency in Free-Text Explanations | Prediction-EXplanation consistency (PEX) extends weight of evidence to compare an explanation's support for a prediction versus its negation. The models are Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Llama-2-13B-chat, and Yi-1.5-9B-chat, initially supervised fine-tuned for classification. | PDF page 9: The approach requires language-model output probabilities and cannot be applied directly to explanations from completely black-box models. | Reviewer interpretation: A proposal must not claim that contrastive consistency metrics or PEX-ranked DPO for free-text explanation refinement are new without distinguishing its contribution from this work. |

## Review boundaries

- Reported findings are author-reported unless independently reproduced; this package did not reproduce them.
- Full-paper extraction reduces abstract-only errors but does not guarantee that every methodological detail was captured.
- The two short evidence passages per paper are audit anchors, not substitutes for the complete paper.
- The candidate gap remains provisional until a broader database search beyond the ACL Anthology comparison set is completed.
